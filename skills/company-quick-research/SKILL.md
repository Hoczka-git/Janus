# Company Quick Research

Research a publicly traded company using current public sources.

## Workflow

1. Identify the company, ticker, exchange and reporting currency.
2. Search for the company's official investor-relations page.
3. Find the latest annual/quarterly financial results.
4. Find important company announcements from the requested period.
5. Find current share price and market capitalization.
6. Use reputable secondary sources only for context.
7. Verify important claims against primary sources whenever possible.
8. Do not rely on search-result snippets for material claims.

## Fact discipline

Classify important statements as:

- FACT — directly supported by a source.
- ESTIMATE — calculated or assumed.
- INTERPRETATION — analytical judgment.

Never invent missing information.

If a fact cannot be verified, say:
"unverified" or "data unavailable".

## Financial analysis

Where available report:

- revenue
- revenue growth
- EBIT / operating profit
- net profit
- operating cash flow
- free cash flow
- cash
- debt / net cash
- margins
- R&D or development spending

Always state the financial reporting period.

## Valuation

Report, where meaningful:

- share price
- market capitalization
- P/E
- EV/EBIT
- EV/EBITDA
- FCF yield

Always state the date of the market data and the period of the financial data.

Do not silently mix current market data with stale financial data.

## Business

Briefly assess:

- business model
- main revenue sources
- competitive advantages
- moat
- growth drivers
- major dependencies
- key risks

## Recent developments

Focus on material developments affecting:

- financial results
- guidance
- products or pipeline
- management
- regulation
- capital allocation
- valuation
- investment thesis

## Output

Produce a concise memo:

1. Verdict
2. Executive summary
3. Recent developments
4. Business quality
5. Financials
6. Growth / pipeline
7. Valuation
8. Key risks
9. What would change the thesis?
10. Sources

Cite important claims with source IDs such as [1], [2], [3].

Prefer primary sources.

Do not provide personalized financial advice.
